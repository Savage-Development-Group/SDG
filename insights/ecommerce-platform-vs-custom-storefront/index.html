E-commerce strategy

E-commerce platform vs. custom storefront.

The best platform is the one that supports the catalog, customer journey, operations, and team without introducing more custom work than the business can sustain.

Published October 4, 2026 · 7 minute read

Start with the catalog

Product count alone is not enough. Consider variants, bundles, personalization, subscriptions, inventory sources, wholesale rules, pricing, digital goods, and product relationships. Complexity here influences nearly every platform decision.

Hosted platforms optimize for common commerce

A mature hosted platform is usually the fastest route when standard products, checkout, payments, tax, shipping, discounts, and administration cover the core operation. The ecosystem can add proven capabilities without owning all infrastructure.

Headless changes the presentation layer

A headless storefront separates the customer-facing experience from the commerce engine. It can support distinctive content and interaction, but adds integration, deployment, preview, and maintenance concerns. Use it when those benefits are concrete.

Custom commerce requires a strong reason

Fully custom development may fit unusual marketplaces, complex pricing, specialized configuration, or commerce embedded inside a larger product. Payments and security should still rely on proven providers wherever possible.

Do not ignore store operations

Product entry, order review, refunds, customer support, fulfillment, inventory, and reporting determine whether the store remains usable after launch. Evaluate the administrative experience alongside the storefront.

A practical selection process

  1. Map products, buyers, checkout, fulfillment, and returns.
  2. List essential integrations and ownership constraints.
  3. Test the simplest capable platform against real scenarios.
  4. Customize only where the experience or operation earns it.
