Software strategy

Custom web app vs. off-the-shelf software.

The right question is not which approach is more impressive. It is which one solves the workflow responsibly at the current stage of the business.

Published October 4, 2026 · 7 minute read

When existing software is the responsible choice

Choose an established product when the workflow is common, configuration covers most needs, speed matters more than differentiation, and the subscription cost is reasonable. Payroll, bookkeeping, email, and basic appointment scheduling are common examples.

Using a mature tool also transfers maintenance, security, and compliance responsibilities to a provider equipped to handle them.

When custom development becomes rational

A custom application can earn its cost when the workflow is central to the business and existing tools create persistent friction. Strong signals include repeated data entry, disconnected systems, special permissions, customer experiences competitors cannot support, or manual work that grows with revenue.

Compare total workflow cost

Subscription price is only one cost. Include implementation, migration, staff time, workarounds, duplicate entry, integration services, errors, training, and the opportunity cost of a poor customer experience.

Consider ownership carefully

Custom software offers control, but control carries responsibility. Hosting, monitoring, security updates, backups, support, documentation, and future changes need an owner. A realistic plan includes the product after launch.

A hybrid path is often best

Custom does not mean rebuilding commodity infrastructure. A focused product can connect proven services for payments, email, identity, accounting, or storage while keeping the distinctive workflow under your control.

A practical decision rule

  • Configure first when the process is standard.
  • Integrate when the tools work but the handoffs do not.
  • Build when the workflow itself is strategic and the cost of friction is durable.
