What does a custom business website cost?

The honest answer depends less on page count than on how much strategy, content, proof, functionality, integration, and uncertainty the project contains.

Published October 4, 2026 · 8 minute read

Price follows responsibility

A brochure site using finished copy and a clear visual direction is a different project from a lead-generation site that needs positioning, service architecture, case studies, photography, CRM integration, analytics, and an ongoing content system.

The main cost drivers

  • Strategy: audience research, offer definition, competitive review, and page planning.
  • Content: copywriting, editing, photography, illustration, and migration.
  • Design: custom visual direction, component depth, and responsive states.
  • Functionality: forms, search, accounts, payments, calculators, or custom workflows.
  • Integrations: CRM, scheduling, email, analytics, or operational systems.
  • Governance: who can edit what, approval needs, accessibility, privacy, and compliance.

Why ranges are more useful than instant quotes

An early range can confirm whether an approach fits the available investment. A responsible fixed scope comes after the team understands what must be produced, what already exists, and which uncertainties need resolution.

Separate launch scope from future ideas

A focused first release can establish the core customer journey, priority services, proof, and lead capture. Lower-priority tools or content can follow when they have a clear reason to exist.

Evaluate value and operating cost

Consider the cost of the current site: poor-fit leads, unclear offers, slow editing, lost search visibility, manual handoffs, and credibility gaps. Also include hosting, domains, software subscriptions, maintenance, and ongoing content in the new-site plan.

A good project brief answers four questions: Who is the site for? What should they understand? What should they do next? What evidence will help them trust the answer?
